summary: "Can individual North Atlantic right whales be distinguished by their upcalls? And what does this mean for the impacts of increasing anthropogenic noise on their ability to not only detect, but also recognize each other? Using BirdNET embeddings, careful denoising, and a noise “control” dataset, we demonstrate that upcalls indeed encode individual identity, and show that current measures likely underestimate the impacts of noise on whale communication."
